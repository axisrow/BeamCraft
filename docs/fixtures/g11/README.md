# G11 accuracy fixtures, set version 1

Contributor-original, BeamCraft contributors, 2026-11-01, SPDX `MPL-2.0`. Task G11, issue #490.

These 50 descriptions check that the real plate, member and fastener builders stay accurate on
inputs where binary64 arithmetic is most likely to fail. They are test descriptions, not saved
models, a profile catalogue or a new engine contract. No dimension, value or name comes from
another kernel, a commercial detailing product, a vendor catalogue, a national table or a client
model. The bolt dimensions are invented round numbers and are not a standard's.

| Class | Count | What it checks |
|-------|-------|----------------|
| `mesh` | 25 | Rounded-mesh cross-checks. A builder's volume, the exact-sum volume enclosure of its certified mesh and the exact reference agree: rectangles and contours with non-dyadic decimals, every parametric section family sharp and four with radii, rectangle profiles, skew axes, and the washer, head, shank and nut of two bolts |
| `subnormal` | 8 | Mass at the bottom of the binary64 range: from the smallest admitted plate to the largest, density from the smallest subnormal to the cap, and a zero density |
| `far` | 8 | Very large coordinates: placements up to the F04 cap of 1e9 mm, a half turn, element-local contours and axes near ±1e9 mm, and a 0.5 x 0.25 mm section there |
| `cancel` | 9 | Cancellation-heavy inputs: thin walls on 1e6 mm sections, sliver triangles near and far from the origin, a 1e6 mm plate at its minimum thickness, a 1e6 mm axis with a 0.5 mm section, and axes on both sides of the near-vertical frame threshold |

The four G03 `unverified` large-translation cases (`docs/fixtures/g03`) could not run because
there was no placement adapter. Their G11 counterparts run through the production world map:
`g11.far.plate_translated`, `plate_half_turn` and `plate_at_negative_cap`. The G03 files are frozen
and unchanged.

## Files

| Path | Author and source | Use |
|------|-------------------|-----|
| `cases.tsv` | BeamCraft contributors; original synthetic inputs, set 1 | One case per line: id, class, body, parameters, density, translation, rotation |
| `expectations.tsv` | Generated by `beamcraft-fixtures g11` from the inputs alone | Outcome, reference kind, curved flag, volume, mass, section area, axis length, world bounds, world centroid |
| `manifest.tsv` | Generated | Byte length and SHA-256 of the two tables |
| This README | BeamCraft contributors | Format, derivations, reproduction, results and limits |

The existing `ATTRIBUTION.md` entry for `docs/fixtures/**` covers these values. All files are
UTF-8 without BOM, with LF line endings. TSV means tab-separated columns with one case per line;
lines that start with `#` are comments. Every input is a decimal token that rounds to the nearest
binary64 value, and the inputs are those binary64 values. Units are mm, kg/m3 and kg.

**Parameters.**

- A plate is `points=x,y;x,y;... thickness=t`: a counter-clockwise contour in the element-local
  plane `z = 0`. The thickness is laid off towards `+Z` (the default `top` side).
- A member is `<profile>=values start=x,y,z end=x,y,z`. The profile is `rectangle=w,h` or a
  section family token with its values in the family's stored order.
- A bolt component is `part=washer|head|shank|nut diameter= head=af,h nut=af,h washer=od,id,t
  length=`. It is built with a washer under the head.
- The rotation is `identity` or `half_turn_z`, the quaternion `(0, 0, 1, 0)`, which is exact.

**References.** Each reference cell is `fraction decimal`. The fraction is the exact rational in
lowest terms. The decimal is the same value rounded to 21 significant digits, which is what the
harness parses. A row marked `exact` is fully rational. A row marked `bounded` uses π to 64
decimals or a square root bounded below within 2^-200, so its error is below 1e-55 relative. A
`curved` row is the ideal circle or arc that the kernel approximates by a polygon. `-` means not
applicable.

## Derivations

All references are exact rational evaluations of the binary64 inputs (`tools/fixtures/src/g11.rs`
with `src/rational.rs`). Nothing is read from the kernel, the engine or any production helper.

- **Plate.** The area is the exact shoelace sum `A = 1/2 Σ (x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)`. The volume
  is `A t`. The centroid is `(Σ (x_i + x_{i+1}) c_i / 6A, Σ (y_i + y_{i+1}) c_i / 6A, t/2)`, where
  `c_i` is the cross term. The bounds are the contour's extremes and `[0, t]`. The world values
  apply the half turn `(x, y, z) -> (-x, -y, z)`, then the translation.
- **Member.** The section areas are the closed forms of the families with `K = 1 - π/4`:
  - I: `2 b tf + (h - 2 tf) tw + 4 K r²`; channel the same with `2 K r²`;
  - angle: `t (h + b - t) + K r²`;
  - RHS: `2 t (h + b - 2 t) - 4 K (r² - r_i²)` with `r_i = max(r - t, 0)`, written without the
    difference of two near-equal areas;
  - CHS: `π t (d - t)`;
  - cold-formed families: centre-line length times `t`, plus `count · K (r² - (r + t)²)` for
    radii above zero.

  The axis length is `sqrt(Σ (end_i - start_i)²)`, exact when the sum is a perfect square. The
  volume is `area · length`. The centroid of a doubly symmetric section (rectangle, I, RHS, CHS)
  is the axis midpoint, then the placement.
- **Mass.** `V · ρ / 1e9`. A positive-density mass below `2^-1022`, the smallest normal binary64,
  is expected as `UNSUPPORTED_GEOMETRY`: F06 `GEO_NUMERIC_UNCERTAIN`, corrected by this task.
  The generator refuses a case whose mass lies within a factor 4 of that floor, so the expected
  outcome never depends on rounding.
- **Bolt.** A hexagon across flats `s` has area `(√3/2) s²`. A disc of diameter `d` has area
  `π d²/4`. The head and nut volumes are `area · height`. The shank is a disc times the length.
  The washer is the disc difference times the thickness.

## Independent harness and comparison rule

`crates/steel/tests/g11_accuracy.rs` runs `create_plate`, `create_straight_member` and
`kernel::fastener::build`. Placed meshes go through `SolidMesh::rotated_by` and
`TriMesh::transformed`. The harness then measures the meshes with the G06 `measure`.

**Rules.**

- **Quantities** pass when `|a - e| <= 1e-6 |e|`.
- **Lengths, bounds and centroids** pass when `|a - e| <= 0.001 mm + 1e-6 |e|`. This is the F04
  section 10 rule, CR-TOL-001.
- **The volume enclosure of a certified mesh** must satisfy the quantity rule plus the
  volume-rounding bound: the surface area times one ulp of the largest coordinate. That bound
  covers rounding the vertices to binary64.
- **A curved section's mesh** is compared with the builder's own polygon volume. The engineering
  volume comes from the exact section area and is compared with the reference.
- **A curved bolt part** (a polygon on its circles) must lie within `π · Σ diameters · max
  sagitta · height` of the circle.

The largest relative error of each class is printed and pinned, so a loss of accuracy fails long
before the rule does.

## Results (2026-11-01)

| Class | Quantities, largest relative error | Mesh volume, largest relative error |
|-------|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| `mesh` | 3.3e-14 (rounded RHS centroid) | 9.5e-15 (sharp I) |
| `subnormal` | 0 (every admitted mass and volume exact) | 0 |
| `far` | 2.4e-16 (half-turn plate centroid) | 1.4e-7 (0.5 x 0.25 mm section near 1e9 mm: vertex rounding, inside its bound) |
| `cancel` | 1.0e-10 (RHS 999999.9 x 999999.7 x 0.4: about four digits cancel) | 1.2e-10 (CHS 999999.9 x 0.4) |

**Defect found and fixed.** A positive density that gave a subnormal mass was reported as a
`current` mass. At 1 mm3 and 1e-310 kg/m3 that mass is about 1e-319 kg, with a relative error near
1e-5, ten times the comparison rule. `beamcraft_kernel::measure::analytic_mass_kg` now refuses any
positive-density mass below the normal range, as it already refused zero. The density amendment
of F04 records the corrected sentence. No F06 constant changed, so `tolerance_policy` stays 3.

**Recorded, not changed.** A 0.2 mm wall on a 999999.9 mm RHS or CHS passes the member's
minimum-dimension check, whose scale is the section half-diagonal. It is then refused by the G05
outline admission (`ContoursTooClose`), whose scale is the outline's own size, about twice as
large. The two rules follow their contracts, so the set uses a 0.4 mm wall. Whether the two
scales should agree is a contract question for F06 section 4, outside G11.

## Reproduction

```sh
cargo run -p beamcraft-fixtures -- g11 verify --dir docs/fixtures/g11
cargo test -p beamcraft-fixtures --test g11
cargo test -p steel --test steel -- g11_accuracy:: --nocapture
```

To change the set, edit `tools/fixtures/src/g11.rs`, raise `SET_VERSION`, then run
`cargo run -p beamcraft-fixtures -- g11 generate --out docs/fixtures/g11`.

## Limits

These are native results on the CI targets. They do not show WASM or cross-platform
conformance. They do not certify arbitrary solids, cut or holed bodies, bent plates or sweeps.
Curved references hold the polygon to its stated chord rule. They do not certify the arc
approximation beyond it. The world map tested is the rigid placement used by the views and
queries; no other consumer is claimed.
